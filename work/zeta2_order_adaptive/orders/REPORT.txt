Exact finite experiments with genuine CMF multiplication orders
=============================================================

Status: NUMERICAL_CANDIDATE. No inference of a uniform arithmetic bound
is made from these finite tests.

Reproduce from the project root:

  work/.venv_4f3/bin/python work/zeta2_order_adaptive/orders/adaptive_paths.py 20 40 80 160 320 640
  work/.venv_4f3/bin/python work/zeta2_order_adaptive/orders/extract_witnesses.py 20 40 80 160 320 640

The first command resumes from complete per-depth JSONL checkpoints.

Experiment
----------
The native pFq(4,3,1) field has initial point (1,1,1,0,1,1,1),
direction (10,9,11,-11,2,1,3), forward orientation, dimension 3,
selected column 0, and initial rows (0,2,-1) and (2,0,0). These
give the numerator and denominator of the requested approximant.

For each depth, 89 two-block paths START -> cut -> endpoint were tested.
Within each block the coordinate order is [x0,x1,x2,y0,y1,y2,x3].
Cuts use upper progress (t,t,t)N, t in {3,4,6,8,9}, or (10,9,11)N;
lower progress in {(0,0,0),(1,0,1),(1,1,2),(2,1,3)}N; and negative
progress -mN, m in {0,3,6,9}. Only regular feasible cuts are retained.
Every suffix contains at least 2N negative steps. Moving a negative
prefix ahead of the remaining positive/lower blocks changes the actual
native multiplication order; these are not merely changed readout shifts.

For prefix A and suffix B, the certificate for row r is the rational gcd
of the three terms (R A)[r,i] B[i,0], i=0,1,2. Their sum is not used to
improve the certificate. The exact incoming rows are retained, including
all cancellation internal to each whole block. Every A B was checked
against the complete endpoint matrix, all nine entries, at every depth.

This is a decomposition certificate. The endpoint approximant, its true
reduced denominator, and its true delta do not change with the order.

Prime-dependent choice genuinely helps the tested certificate
-------------------------------------------------------------
At N=80, the complete sets of maximizing paths at p=2 and p=29 are
disjoint. The path with upper cut (3,3,3)N and all other cut coordinates
zero gives row bounds (-19,1) at p=2, but (-6,0) at p=29. The full
positive/lower preparation followed by 9N negative steps before the cut
gives (-27,-10) at p=2, but (-4,0) at p=29. The optimal common bounds
are respectively -19 and -4. This is strict incompatibility, not a choice
between tied maxima.

The prime-adaptive common certificate improves on the best single path
by exact factors 147649788 at N=80, 191016486453 at N=160,
62736532700183576976227 at N=320, and
4689573862548643387295944573533940705 at N=640.

Row-dependent maxima also differ, with an important limitation
-------------------------------------------------------------
At N=80 and p=883, cut (6,6,6,-9,2,1,3)N gives row bounds (-2,-2),
whereas cut (3,3,3,0,0,0,0)N gives (-3,0). The individual maxima are
(-2,0), and no tested path achieves both. This persists in analogous
witnesses at N=20,40,160,320,640 (primes 223,449,1777,3527,7043).

Nevertheless the shared divisor bound is -2 in that example. At N40,p491
there IS a strict path-only gain: cuts (3,3,3,-3;0,0,0)N and
(3,3,3,0;0,0,0)N give (-1,-2) and (-2,0), so independent row choice
raises the common bound from -2 to -1. The old normalization already
supplies that factor. Across every tested depth the two common certificates
agree AFTER adjoining the old arithmetic normalization. The additional
factor beyond it is exactly 1. The raw gain factor is 491 at N40 and 1
at the other five depths.

Large-prime holdouts and next region
-----------------------------------
At N=320 the new primes beyond the old six-shell normalization, with
p^2>50N, are 139,149,167,281,283. At N=640 they are
269,337,439,443,463,491,521,523,557,563. Every gain is one power of p.
Every one belongs to a periodic lift of the original six chambers.

At each of those two depths, the intersection of maximizing path sets
for all those large primes consists exactly of the full-preparation cut
(10,9,11,0,2,1,3)N, followed by the complete negative x3 block. Hence
these particular gains require no prime-dependent order in this grid.

The next useful finite search region is refined unequal upper-coordinate
cuts and lower-coordinate progress, with at least 2N negative suffix
steps, at N=1280. Focus on primes near lifted-shell boundaries and on
primes where the retained component bound still differs from actual
content. Require disjoint maximizing sets before claiming incompatible
prime choices, and compare max_path min_row against min_row max_path
before claiming a common-divisor benefit from row adaptation.
